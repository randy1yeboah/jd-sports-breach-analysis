# JD Sports data breach analysis

An incident analysis I wrote for a cybersecurity class on the JD Sports breach disclosed in January 2023. It covers what was exposed, how the attackers likely got in, how the company responded, and which laws apply.

## What happened

In January 2023, JD Sports disclosed that attackers had accessed records tied to about 10 million customers. The data came from online orders placed between November 2018 and October 2020 across several of its brands: JD, Size?, Millets, Blacks, Scotts and MilletSport.

The exposed records included names, addresses, email addresses, phone numbers and partial payment card information.

## How they likely got in

JD Sports did not release full technical details. Based on how similar breaches happen, the likely openings were unpatched systems, outdated security protocols, reused credentials, or a poorly secured database holding old order records.

## Impact

Affected customers face a higher risk of phishing and identity fraud, since attackers now have enough personal detail to write convincing scam messages. The company also lost some customer trust.

## Response

JD Sports reported the breach to the UK Information Commissioner's Office (ICO), and public disclosure followed soon after.

## What went wrong

- Customer order data was kept for years, which widened what attackers could take.
- Encryption and layered defenses weren't strong enough to limit the damage.
- Clear, fast communication with customers matters during a breach, and this case shows the cost when data sits exposed for a long time before anyone notices.

## Recommendations

- Set and enforce data retention limits so old order data gets deleted
- Run regular security audits and keep systems patched
- Strengthen encryption for stored customer data
- Train employees on phishing and basic security practices

## Legal side

The breach falls under the UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018, which require companies to protect personal data and report breaches to the ICO. To stay compliant, JD Sports needs regular data protection audits, better oversight of how customer data is stored, and stronger handling of customer data rights.

## Looking ahead

Threats like AI-written phishing and attacks on old, unpatched systems are growing. Retailers holding years of customer data need security that gets updated as those threats change.
